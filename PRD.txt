 Smart Door-to-Door Waste Collection Scheduling System: Product Requirements Document (PRD) Document type: Product Requirements Document (PRD) | Version: 1.0 (Draft) | Date: 1 November 2026 | Prepared by: Tech titans| Based on: MRD v2 (research-based) | Context: College project 1. Product Overview 1.1 Purpose. To define what the Smart Door-to-Door Waste Collection Scheduling System must do, for whom, and how we will know it works. 1.2 Problem. Missed household pickups, irregular timings, lack of collection proof and poor communication reduce citizen trust. 1.3 Solution. A mobile application with role-based screens that helps a municipality allocate workers to ward routes, verify pickups with QR and GPS, track vehicles, notify residents, handle complaints with photos, monitor worker attendance and workload, reschedule during disruptions, and produce monthly ward coverage reports. Item Description Product type Mobile application (Flutter) with a Node.js backend and a PostgreSQL database with PostGIS Users Residents, sanitary workers, ward officers, municipal administrators Scope Pilot-scale student prototype using simulated or small pilot data [A] Assumed pilot size About 5 wards and 2,000 households [A] Platform Android 8 or later [A]; iOS optional 2. Feature Specifications 5.1 Registration and household verification (FR-22) A resident registers with name, phone number, address and ward. The household stays Pending until the ward officer verifies it. On verification the system issues a unique QR code for the household, which is printed as a sticker or card [A]. Edge cases: duplicate registration for one address is rejected; a household that moves is deactivated by the officer; apartment buildings are handled as one household per flat, each with its own QR code [A]. 5.2 Ward, route and worker allocation (FR-01, FR-02) The administrator creates wards and assigns a ward officer to each. The officer creates routes and assigns verified households to them. The officer allocates one worker and one vehicle per route per day, and sets the collection window for each route. A household belongs to one ward and one route at a time. A worker sees only the route assigned for the day. 5.3 Pickup confirmation (FR-03 to FR-06) The worker opens the route list and scans each household&apos;s QR code. The app records time and GPS and shows a confirmation. If the GPS position is farther than the allowed distance from the household (default 50 m, configurable [A]), the pickup is saved as Flagged for officer review. If a pickup is not possible, the worker picks a reason (household not available, waste not segregated, access blocked, other). If a code cannot be scanned (damaged or missing), the officer can confirm manually with a mandatory reason; the action is logged. A second scan of the same household on the same day is ignored and logged. Offline scans are stored on the phone and synced when the network returns. 5.4 Missed pickups and re-collection (FR-07 to FR-09) At the end of each route&apos;s collection window, every household without a confirmation or a reason is marked Missed automatically. A resident can also report a missed pickup for the current or previous collection day. Each miss creates a re-collection task. The officer assigns it to a worker, with a target of 24 hours [T]. The task is closed by a QR scan on re-collection. Pickup status values: Scheduled, Collected, Flagged, Not possible (with reason), Missed, Re-collection scheduled, Re-collected. 5.5 Notifications and live tracking (FR-10 to FR-12) Push notifications are the main channel. SMS is sent for the most important alerts as a fallback. While a route is active, the worker&apos;s phone sends vehicle location at a fixed interval (about 30 seconds [T]). Residents see the vehicle only on their own route&apos;s active window. Worker location is shared only during an active shift or route. Missed-pickup and reschedule alerts stay on. 5.6 Complaints with photos (FR-13 to FR-15) A complaint has a category (for example missed pickup, irregular timing, worker behaviour, spill or litter, other), a description, the household location and up to 3 photos. The system sends it to the officer of the resident&apos;s ward. Status values: Received, In progress, Resolved, Reopened. The officer adds a remark when changing status. The resident can give feedback and reopen an unresolved complaint. Photos are compressed and limited in size before upload [A]. 5.7 Worker attendance and workload (FR-16, FR-17) The worker marks shift start and end; the app records time and GPS. The officer sees, per worker and day, households assigned, collected, missed and re-collected, and attendance status. 5.8 Automatic rescheduling (FR-18, FR-19) The officer records a disruption: worker absent, vehicle breakdown, holiday, road blockage or weather. The system proposes a reschedule using fixed rules: first reassign the route to an available worker and vehicle; if none is available, move the route to the next collection slot. After the officer approves, affected residents are notified. The original schedule stays linked to the new one so reports show both. 5.9 Monthly ward coverage reports (FR-20, FR-21) Each report shows, per ward and month: households scheduled, collected, missed, re-collected, coverage percentage, and complaints received and resolved. Officers see their own ward; administrators see all wards and can compare them. PDF export is available. 6. Key User Flows 6.1 Worker daily flow Log in and mark shift start. Open today&apos;s route and start the route (vehicle location sharing begins). Scan each household&apos;s QR code, or record a reason. Finish the route and mark shift end; any offline scans sync. 6.2 Missed pickup flow The collection window ends; the system marks unconfirmed households as Missed. Residents and the officer are notified; re-collection tasks are created. The officer assigns a worker; the worker collects and scans the code. The task closes and the resident is notified. 6.3 Complaint flow The resident submits a complaint with photos. The ward officer receives it and updates the status with a remark. The resident is notified and can accept the result or reopen the complaint. 6.4 Disruption flow The officer records a disruption for a route and date. The system proposes a reschedule; the officer approves or edits it. Affected residents and the assigned worker are notified. 7. Could items are deferred. 11. Testing and Acceptance Functional testing: every acceptance criterion in Section 4 is run as a test case with simulated data (about 5 wards and 2,000 households [A]). Field simulation: walk or drive a short route with real GPS and QR stickers to test scan distance, offline sync and vehicle tracking. Usability testing: at least 5 users per role complete the main tasks; record time and errors. Security checks: attempts to reach another role&apos;s screens or data must fail in 100% of tests. Release acceptance: all Must requirements pass their acceptance criteria, with no open critical defects. 12. Success Metrics All targets are [T] proposed targets. Dependencies, Risks, and Open Questions 13.1 Dependencies Simulated or pilot data for wards, routes and households An SMS gateway account with trial credits Android phones with GPS for testing QR sticker or card printing for pilot households 13.2 Key Risks Risk Mitigation Workers skip scanning because it slows them down (reported in other cities, see MRD) Fast scan screen, offline mode, quick reason buttons, officer spot checks Wrong or mismatched household data (reported in other cities, see MRD) Officer verification; QR linked to a system record, not a typed house number GPS inaccuracy flags valid pickups Configurable distance; manual override with reason Poor connectivity Offline scanning; SMS fallback Privacy concerns about location tracking Track only during shifts; role-based access; minimum data Scope too large for the semester MoSCoW priorities and a clear MVP 13.3 Open Questions What collection window and frequency should the pilot routes use? What allowed GPS distance works best in dense streets (50 m is only a starting value)? How will apartment buildings and shared collection points be modelled? Which SMS gateway and which local language will the pilot use? Who will print and place the QR stickers in the pilot? Can any real ward data be used, or will all data be simulated?
