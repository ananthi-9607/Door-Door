Executive Summary 1.1 Purpose. This MRD explains why a door-to-door waste collection scheduling system is needed, how existing solutions compare, what our project should do differently, and how success will be measured. The scope is a pilot-scale college prototype. 1.2 Key research findings [V] India&apos;s Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban) 2.0 aims at 100% door-to-door collection of segregated waste from each household [S4]. A progress table showed 97% of wards (83,435 of 86,284) reporting door-to-door collection as of December 2020 [S3]. [AN] This is a ward-level count, so it does not show whether every household is served on time. [V] Several cities use QR codes on houses that workers scan at pickup, and some add GPS on vehicles: Mangaluru [S12], Rourkela [S11], Nagpur [S13], Amritsar [S17] and Mira-Bhayandar [S18]. West Bengal announced a similar plan in July 2026 [S10]. [V] Reported problems include workers finding scanning slow in Dehradun [S14], mismatched house numbers in Guwahati [S15], and residents describing no fixed timetable in Guwahati [S16]. [V] The national Swachhata app lets citizens report cleanliness problems with photos [S5, S7]. [AN] The pages we reviewed do not describe household-level pickup verification or collection scheduling. [AN] The opportunity for this project is a simple, low-cost app that joins pickup proof, resident communication, complaints, worker monitoring and rescheduling in one workflow. 1.3 Project at a glance Item Description Product Mobile app for municipalities to manage collection routes, verify pickups with QR and GPS, track vehicles, handle complaints, notify residents and monitor workers Planned technology Flutter app, Node.js backend, PostgreSQL with PostGIS, GPS, push and SMS notifications Users Residents, sanitary workers, ward officers, municipal administrators Scope Pilot-scale student prototype using simulated or small pilot data [A] Context assumed An urban local body in India, based on the user roles listed [A] 2. Market Background and Existing Solutions 2.1 Market background Fact Label and source The world generated about 2.01 billion tonnes of municipal solid waste in 2016, projected to reach 3.40 billion tonnes by 2050 [V] [S1] Waste generation in South Asia is expected to double by 2050 [V] [S2] SBM-U 2.0 aims at complete door-to-door collection of segregated waste from each household or premise [V] [S4] As of December 2020, 83,435 of 86,284 target wards (97%) reported door-to-door collection, and 78% reported source segregation [V] [S3] The Swachhata complaint app was launched on 6 August 2016 for 4,041 statutory towns [V] [S5] West Bengal announced (July 2026) a unique QR code for every urban household and GPS trackers on collection vehicles [V] [S10] [AN] Together these facts show strong policy demand for door-to-door collection and growing use of digital monitoring. They do not give a market size for scheduling software, and we do not estimate one. 2.2 Existing solutions Solution Type What it does (as reported) Source Swachhata-MoHUA National government complaint app Citizens report cleanliness issues with a photo and automatic location; the complaint goes to the city and a sanitary inspector; resolution photo and feedback; complaints can be reopened; dashboards and ward-wise ranking [S5] [S6] [S7] City QR and GPS monitoring (Mangaluru, Rourkela, Nagpur, Amritsar, Mira-Bhayandar) Municipal or contractor systems Houses carry QR codes; workers scan at pickup; some systems add GPS on vehicles, weighing, segregation feedback or a complaint helpline [S11] [S12] [S13] [S17] [S18] WeVOIS Commercial platform, India App guides staff along routes; citizens see schedules and track vehicles in real time (as reported in a news article) [S19] Binimise Commercial platform, India Vendor lists dashboards, vehicle GPS tracking, attendance, task allocation, grievances and alerts for route deviations [S20] Routeware Commercial platform, international Digital service records with date, time and GPS; exceptions with notes and photos; route optimisation; resident app, collection calendar and service requests [S21] [S22] [S23] Waste Samaritan (Bengaluru) Community initiative Collectors used a mobile app and property-based QR codes to rate segregation in 250 geotagged households [S24] 2.3 Reported strengths and limitations Solution Reported strengths Reported limitations or concerns Swachhata-MoHUA Wide national reach; photo and location capture; feedback and reopen option [S5] [S7] [S6] A government usability study found the &quot;latest complaints around you&quot; section showing complaints from five years earlier [S8] [V]. Proposed Solution and Requirements 6.1 Solution Summary A mobile application, with screens for each role, that lets a municipality allocate workers to ward routes, verify each pickup by QR scan and GPS, track vehicles, notify residents, handle complaints with photos, monitor worker attendance and workload, reschedule collection during disruptions, and produce monthly ward coverage reports. The administrator sets up wards; the ward officer creates routes and allocates workers. Residents register; the officer verifies each household; a QR code is issued. The worker follows the route and scans each QR; the app records time and GPS. Households not confirmed by the end of the collection window are marked missed and a re-collection task is created. Residents are notified of pickups, misses, re-collections and reschedules; complaints go to the ward officer. Monthly ward coverage reports are generated for officers and administrators. 6.2 Market Requirements (MKT) ID Market requirement Evidence Priority MKT-01 Residents need to know when collection will happen and whether it happened. Gap G-04 [S16] [S25] M MKT-02 Municipalities need verifiable proof that a household was served. [S12] [S13] M MKT-03 Ward officers need to see missed and pending pickups during the day. Gap G-01 [AN] M MKT-04 Residents need a simple way to report problems with photos and see the status. [S5] [S7] M MKT-05 Scanning must not slow down sanitary workers. Gap G-02 [S14] S MKT-06 Municipalities need fair, data-based monitoring of attendance and workload. [A] S MKT-07 The solution should work on low-cost phones and low bandwidth, with SMS for key alerts. [A] S MKT-08 The app should support English and one local language. Swachhata offers English and six regional languages [S5] C 6.3 Functional Requirements (FR) Acceptance criteria are [T] proposed targets, tested on simulated or pilot data. ID Feature Requirement Priority Acceptance criterion [T] FR-01 Allocation The administrator creates wards; the officer creates routes and assigns households. M An officer sets up a route of 20 households in 5 minutes or less. FR-02 Allocation The officer allocates a worker and vehicle to each route per day; a worker sees only their own route. M In a test with 3 workers, 0 cases of seeing another worker&apos;s route. FR-03 Pickup proof Each verified household has a QR code; the worker scans it to confirm a pickup; time and GPS are stored. M 20 of 20 test scans stored with time and location; confirmation shown within 3 seconds. FR-04 Pickup proof Scans made outside a configurable distance of the household are flagged. M 100% of out-of-range test scans flagged. FR-05 Pickup proof The worker records a reason when pickup is not possible; the officer can confirm manually with a reason. S 100% of manual confirmations appear in the audit log. FR-06 Pickup proof Scanning works offline and syncs later. S 50 offline test scans sync with no loss. FR-07 Missed pickups Households not confirmed by the end of the collection window are flagged as missed. M Flagged within 15 minutes of window end in tests. FR-08 Missed pickups A resident can report a missed pickup; the system creates a re-collection task and notifies the resident. M Task visible to the officer within 1 minute in 100% of test reports. FR-09 Missed pickups The officer tracks and closes re-collection tasks. S Task status matches actual status in all test cases. FR-10 Notifications Push notifications for schedule, pickup done, pickup missed, re-collection and reschedule. M At least 90% of test notifications arrive within 60 seconds. FR-11 Notifications SMS fallback for missed pickup, re-collection and reschedule alerts. S An SMS is sent for each key alert in tests. FR-12 Vehicle tracking The resident sees the live location of the vehicle serving their route during its active window. S Marker refreshes at least every 30 seconds on a stable network. FR-13 Complaints A resident submits a complaint with category, description, location and up to 3 photos. M Submitted within 2 minutes; photos attached in 100% of tests. FR-14 Complaints Complaints go to the ward officer; status changes (Received, In progress, Resolved) notify the resident. M Correct officer in 100% of tests; notification within 60 seconds. FR-15 Complaints A resident can give feedback and reopen a complaint they consider unresolved. S Reopened complaint returns to the officer queue in 100% of tests. FR-16 Worker monitoring A worker marks shift start and end with time and GPS. S Recorded in 100% of test shifts. FR-17 Worker monitoring The officer sees workload per worker (assigned, completed, missed). S Counts match a manual count of test data. FR-18 Rescheduling The officer records a disruption (worker absent, vehicle breakdown, holiday, road blockage). S Visible to the system within 1 minute. FR-19 Rescheduling The system proposes a reschedule using fixed rules and notifies affected residents after approval. S Proposal within 30 seconds; all affected households covered in each test scenario. FR-20 Reports A monthly ward coverage report shows households scheduled, collected, missed, re-collected, and complaints received and resolved. M Totals match source records; generated within 30 seconds for 500 households [A]. FR-21 Reports Reports export to PDF; the administrator can compare wards. C PDF opens correctly; comparison shows all pilot wards. FR-22 Accounts Secure login with role-based access; the officer verifies resident registrations. M Unauthorised access blocked in 100% of tests. FR-23 Accounts An audit log records overrides, reschedules and status changes. S Every such action is logged. 6.4 Non-Functional Requirements (NFR) ID Category Requirement Priority NFR-01 Usability A new resident registers and views the schedule in under 3 minutes without help (test with 5 users).
